# Audience Personas

Phase 0.5 output. Covers the 10 audience angles from the source brief, grounded in the product's actual 9 role-based panels (`docs/TEST_USERS.md`) plus the market research in `content/research/RESEARCH_NOTES.md`. These are working personas for content-planning purposes, not final segmentation — expect Phase 1 to sharpen them once positioning is set.

**The most important addition versus the brief's own list:** the research surfaced a distinct 11th angle — the **Gulf-based Yemeni diaspora investor** — important enough to treat as its own persona rather than folding into "buyer." See `RESEARCH_NOTES.md` §1 for why.

---

## 1. Property Seeker / Buyer (domestic Yemen)

- **Who:** someone in Mukalla, Sana'a, Aden, or another Yemeni city actively looking to buy or rent.
- **Real product surface:** public site search/filter, property detail pages (photos, price, neighborhood stats, calculators), booking a viewing, contact/WhatsApp-style outreach.
- **What they actually care about:** trustworthy pricing (no bait-and-switch), real photos, whether the listing is still available, how to actually arrange a viewing, financing/affordability (the mortgage calculator is directly relevant here).
- **Constraint to respect:** low-bandwidth reality (17.7% national internet penetration) — content reaching this persona needs to work on modest connections and be mobile-first; can't assume desktop or high-bandwidth video consumption.
- **Content angle:** practical, trust-building, local — "how to actually find and view a property," not aspirational lifestyle content.

## 2. Property Owner (domestic, listing to sell or rent)

- **Real product surface:** Seller/Landlord panels — listing creation, active-listing stats, lease management, tenant tracking, rental applications.
- **What they care about:** will this actually get my property seen, is it free/affordable to list, how much control do I keep, what happens with inquiries.
- **Content angle:** demystify the listing process, show the actual dashboard (real UI, not a mockup), address the "will strangers actually see this" trust question directly.

## 3. Real Estate Agent / Broker

- **Real product surface:** Agent panel — property/appointment/booking management, lead handling, agent-performance stats.
- **What they care about:** does this make their existing workflow faster or slower, does it help them close more, will it replace them or empower them (address directly — don't dodge it).
- **Content angle:** workflow/efficiency-focused, "here's your day with Aqar" style walkthroughs; agent-performance dashboard is a genuine hook (agents like seeing their own numbers).

## 4. Agency (multi-agent real-estate business)

- **Real product surface:** Staff/Admin panels — the demo's own "Dar Hadhramaut Real Estate" persona is literally this audience.
- **What they care about:** managing a team, not just one listing; multi-property oversight; revenue/transaction visibility (Staff dashboard's real revenue/booking/transaction stats are directly relevant).
- **Content angle:** "run your agency on Aqar" positioning — the Staff dashboard genuinely supports this narrative with real data, not a mockup.

## 5. Property Manager

- **Real product surface:** Landlord/Tenant panels — lease lifecycle, maintenance request handling, work orders, tenant communication.
- **What they care about:** reducing the operational chaos of maintenance requests and lease admin specifically — this is the most workflow/pain-point-driven persona of the set.
- **Content angle:** "before/after" workflow content — maintenance-request handling is a genuinely strong, concrete pain point Aqar's product visibly addresses (Tenant/Contractor/Landlord panels all connect on this exact workflow).

## 6. Administrator (internal, platform/agency operator)

- **Real product surface:** Admin panel — the full oversight layer across properties, users, branches.
- **Note:** this is closer to an internal/operational audience than an external marketing target — likely most relevant as a *sales* conversation (for an agency evaluating Aqar as their platform) rather than broad content marketing. Flag for Phase 1 to decide whether this deserves dedicated content or folds into the Agency persona (#4).

## 7. Developer / Real-Estate Company (larger business customer)

- **Who:** a real-estate development or larger brokerage company evaluating Aqar as their operating platform, not an individual agent.
- **Real product surface:** the full multi-panel system, team/branch structure, the investment-analytics layer (a differentiator this audience specifically would value — see `COMPETITOR_ANALYSIS.md` on the Aqarmap data-led positioning parallel).
- **Content angle:** more B2B/LinkedIn-appropriate than the individual-agent content above; case-study/capability-focused once real usage exists (per the anti-fabrication rule, cannot use fabricated case studies now — this persona's content depends on Phase 7+ real deployment).

## 8. Potential Business Customer (broader — could include adjacent services)

- **Overlaps significantly with #7.** Distinct mainly in being earlier-funnel/less-defined — "someone who might need this but doesn't know what 'this' is yet." TOFU-stage awareness content territory (Phase 1.5 funnel mapping) more than a distinct persona with distinct product needs.

## 9. Investor / Partner — reframed as **Gulf-based Yemeni diaspora investor** (research-driven addition)

- **Who:** a Yemeni living and working in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, or elsewhere in the Gulf, sending remittances home, and — per real, sourced evidence (`RESEARCH_NOTES.md` §1) — already an active participant in Yemeni real estate demand (diaspora-connected areas show measurably inflated prices, meaning this money is *already* flowing into property).
- **Why this matters more than the brief's generic "investor/partner" framing:** this isn't a hypothetical future segment, it's a demonstrated, currently-active buying behavior that domestic-Yemen-only content won't reach effectively, given the platform/reach realities in `RESEARCH_NOTES.md`.
- **What they actually care about:** buying/managing property in Yemen *remotely* — trust and verification matter even more than for a local buyer (they can't just drive by and look), transaction transparency, being able to communicate fast (WhatsApp — see `RESEARCH_NOTES.md` §2), understanding what they're actually buying without being physically present.
- **Content angle:** "manage your Yemen property from Riyadh/Dubai" style positioning; remote-verification/trust content; content in a Gulf-reachable channel mix (WhatsApp, Instagram, possibly TikTok) distinct from the domestic-Yemen Facebook-led mix.
- **This persona should likely be a top content-marketing priority**, not an afterthought — flagged for Phase 1 to weigh explicitly rather than defaulting to the brief's original ordering.

## 10. General Audience — First-Time Discovery

- **Who:** anyone encountering Aqar for the first time with zero context — a shared Reel, a friend's recommendation, a search result.
- **What they need:** the "What is Aqar?" answer in the first few seconds (ties directly to Phase 2's 3-second-hook research finding) — what it is, who it's for, why it's different, in plain terms, before anything else.
- **Content angle:** this is the audience the brand-introduction flagship piece (`PLAN.md` Phase 7.2) is written for — the single most important "explain the whole thing simply" piece in the initial content pack.

---

## Cross-persona notes

- **Trust is the dominant theme across almost every persona above** — not a coincidence; it matches the MENA-market research finding that audiences here are unusually trust-driven and that social proof outperforms promotional content (`RESEARCH_NOTES.md` §3). Content strategy (Phase 1) should treat "trust-building" as a near-universal secondary objective across pillars, not a single isolated pillar.
- **Mobile-first, low-bandwidth-tolerant production is a constraint for domestic-Yemen-facing personas (1, 2, 5)** specifically, less so for the diaspora persona (9) who has Gulf-level connectivity. Production standards (Phase 2.7) may need two tiers rather than one.
- **Personas 6 and 8 are the weakest/least-distinct** of the ten and are flagged above as likely candidates to fold into adjacent personas during Phase 1, rather than force-fitting ten equally-weighted segments where the real product/market evidence only clearly supports somewhat fewer.
