# Content Strategy

Phase 1.1 (content ecosystem) + 1.4 (pillar framework). This is the central strategy document — `CONTENT_FUNNEL.md` and `CONTENT_STRATEGY_MATRIX.md` (Phase 1.5/1.6) build directly on the pillars defined here.

## 1. The content ecosystem — beyond "social posts"

Per the source brief §3, content needs to do six distinct jobs, not just "post regularly":

| Job | What it means for Aqar | Primary pillar(s) below |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand awareness** | People recognize Aqar, know what it is, know it's different (data/insight-led, per `BRAND_POSITIONING.md`) | Product & Insight |
| **Education** | Real estate knowledge — buying/selling/renting, market concepts, mistakes to avoid | Real Estate Education |
| **Product education** | How Aqar specifically works — search, dashboards, the analytics that differentiate it | Product & Insight |
| **Demonstration** | Show the real product working — screen recordings, walkthroughs | Product & Insight |
| **Entertainment** | Not everything is an ad — myths, surprising facts, relatable scenarios | Real Estate Education (entertainment-adjacent formats) |
| **Trust** | Credibility, honesty about what's real vs. demo, company philosophy | Trust & Credibility |
| **Conversion** | Drive visits/registrations/listings/inquiries | Conversion & Offers |

## 2. The pillar framework

Six pillars, not the source brief's example list applied blindly — this structure reflects what Phase 0's research and this product's actual reality support. **A note on the sourced 40/25/20/15 mix** (`content/research/SOURCES.md` #12, Educational/Social-proof/Behind-the-scenes/Promotional): Aqar cannot fill a "social proof" bucket honestly yet — there are no real customer testimonials or usage stories to draw on (`FEATURE_REALITY_CLASSIFICATION.md` is explicit that the seeded demo data must never be presented as real). That bucket is replaced below with **Trust & Credibility** — content that builds the same kind of confidence social proof would, without fabricating anything, until real user stories genuinely exist (at which point Phase 10-and-beyond iteration should reintroduce a real social-proof pillar).

### Pillar A — Product & Insight
- **Purpose:** show what Aqar actually does, especially the data/insight differentiator (`BRAND_POSITIONING.md`).
- **Audience:** all personas, weighted toward Seeker/Buyer, Owner, Agent, Developer (`PERSONAS.md` #1–4, #7).
- **Business objective:** awareness → consideration; drives registration and listing creation.
- **Tone:** direct, demonstrative, confident without hype.
- **Formats:** screen-recorded UI walkthroughs, feature-spotlight shorts, the investment-analytics panel as a recurring visual hook, dashboard tours per persona.
- **Example topics:** "How Aqar scores a property's investment risk," "What your Aqar dashboard actually shows you," "Search in 10 seconds."
- **Example hooks:** UI-first hook ("watch this get done in two taps" — `content/research/SOURCES.md` #9), data hook ("this property scored 5/10 on risk — here's why").
- **Example CTAs:** "Try the search yourself," "See your own property's numbers."
- **Platform fit:** primary across both tracks (`PLATFORM_STRATEGY.md`); this pillar is where YouTube long-form does the most work (deep walkthroughs).
- **Frequency:** highest of all pillars early on — this is the differentiation story and needs repetition to land.
- **Production complexity:** medium (real screen recording + editing, no fabrication risk since it's just showing real UI).
- **Expected value:** highest — this is the actual differentiator, not a generic value prop.
- **Metrics:** watch time/completion (video), click-through to product, registration conversion.

### Pillar B — Real Estate Education
- **Purpose:** general real-estate knowledge that's useful independent of Aqar — buying/selling/renting guidance, market concepts, common mistakes.
- **Audience:** Seeker/Buyer, Owner, general first-time-discovery audience (`PERSONAS.md` #1, #2, #10).
- **Business objective:** top-of-funnel awareness, positions Aqar as knowledgeable, not just a listings tool.
- **Tone:** helpful, plain-spoken, occasionally entertainment-adjacent (myths, mistakes, surprising facts — per source brief §3's "entertainment" job).
- **Formats:** myth-vs-fact shorts, "3 mistakes when buying in [city]" listicle videos, documentation/verification explainer content (directly relevant given Yemen's property-documentation realities).
- **Example topics:** "What to check before paying a deposit," "Common Yemeni property-document mistakes," "Renting vs. buying in [city] right now."
- **Example hooks:** mistake-based, myth-vs-reality, curiosity ("the one document most buyers forget to check").
- **Example CTAs:** "Save this before you go house-hunting," "Follow for more," soft product mentions rather than hard CTAs (this pillar's job is trust/reach, not direct conversion).
- **Platform fit:** primary on Facebook (Track A) and Instagram/TikTok (Track B) — the most shareable, algorithm-friendly pillar.
- **Frequency:** second-highest — this is the reach-and-trust engine.
- **Production complexity:** low-medium (mostly narration/text/graphics, minimal product-specific asset dependency).
- **Expected value:** high for reach and trust; lower direct-conversion attribution (it's a funnel-top pillar, measure accordingly — see `CONTENT_FUNNEL.md`).
- **Metrics:** reach, shares, saves, follower growth.

### Pillar C — Trust & Credibility
- **Purpose:** build the confidence that would normally come from social proof, honestly, without fabricating testimonials or usage numbers (Non-Negotiable Rule #1).
- **Audience:** all personas — trust is named as a near-universal cross-persona theme in `PERSONAS.md`.
- **Business objective:** overcome the specific trust barriers named in personas — "will strangers actually see my listing," "can I trust a remote purchase" (diaspora persona), "is this data real."
- **Tone:** transparent, specific, quietly confident — explicitly willing to say what's real vs. demo (per `FEATURE_REALITY_CLASSIFICATION.md`) rather than glossing over it, which is itself a trust signal.
- **Formats:** "how verification actually works" explainers, company/product-philosophy pieces, honest "what's live today vs. what's coming" content (a genuinely differentiated move — most competitors don't do this).
- **Example topics:** "How we verify a listing," "What Aqar's investment score is actually based on," "Why we're honest about what's still in progress."
- **Example hooks:** transparency-as-hook ("most platforms won't tell you this, we will"), direct address of a named objection.
- **Example CTAs:** "See how it works," low-pressure — this pillar's job is confidence, not urgency.
- **Platform fit:** works everywhere but is the natural home for longer-form YouTube/website content, since trust-building benefits from depth.
- **Frequency:** lower volume, higher depth — not a high-cadence pillar.
- **Production complexity:** low (mostly honest explanation, minimal production dependency) but high care required (this is the pillar most sensitive to Non-Negotiable Rule #1 violations).
- **Expected value:** high for overcoming the specific objections named in `PERSONAS.md`, especially for the diaspora persona (#9).
- **Metrics:** comments/replies (qualitative trust signal), direct-message inquiry rate.

### Pillar D — Local & Diaspora Life
- **Purpose:** the track-specific connective-tissue content — neighborhood/market context for Track A, remote-ownership life for Track B.
- **Audience:** Track A: Seeker/Buyer/Owner in specific cities. Track B: diaspora investor persona specifically (`PERSONAS.md` #9).
- **Business objective:** relevance and identity — "this is made for people like me," which the research flags as more valuable than generic pan-Arab content for this audience.
- **Tone:** warm, specific, place-based (real Yemeni cities/neighborhoods, not generic).
- **Formats:** neighborhood spotlights (using the product's real neighborhood-stats feature), diaspora-specific content ("managing your Yemen property from Riyadh"), market-update shorts.
- **Example topics:** "What living in [Mukalla neighborhood] is actually like," "Buying property in Yemen while you live in the Gulf — what changes."
- **Example hooks:** neighborhood/lifestyle-context hooks (`content/research/SOURCES.md` #3 category).
- **Example CTAs:** explore properties in [neighborhood], see the diaspora-focused landing content.
- **Platform fit:** Track A → Facebook/Instagram; Track B → Instagram/WhatsApp.
- **Frequency:** medium, steady.
- **Production complexity:** medium (benefits from real local knowledge/research per-neighborhood, not fabricated).
- **Expected value:** medium-high for the specific track/persona it targets; not a broad-reach pillar by design.
- **Metrics:** engagement rate within the specific geo/persona targeting, not raw reach.

### Pillar E — Company / Brand
- **Purpose:** the "who's actually behind this" story — development process, philosophy, milestones, honestly scoped to what's real (Non-Negotiable Rule #1 — no fabricated milestones/awards).
- **Audience:** general first-time-discovery, B2B personas (Developer/Agency).
- **Business objective:** longer-term brand-equity building, not immediate conversion.
- **Tone:** understated, genuine — avoid the "generic motivational founder content" trap named in source brief §37.
- **Formats:** low-frequency, occasional — this is not a high-cadence pillar for an early-stage product with limited real milestones yet.
- **Example topics:** "Why we built the investment-analytics feature," "What 'Novixa Aqar' actually means" (brand-name explainer — genuinely useful for the first-time-discovery persona).
- **Frequency:** lowest of all pillars — quality/timing over cadence; don't force content here just to fill a calendar slot.
- **Metrics:** brand-recall proxies (search volume for "Aqar"/"Novixa Aqar" over time — a real, trackable metric once there's baseline data).

### Pillar F — Conversion & Offers
- **Purpose:** the direct-response pillar — property listings, sign-up pushes, specific CTAs.
- **Audience:** bottom-of-funnel across all personas.
- **Business objective:** direct conversion (registrations, listings created, inquiries).
- **Tone:** clear, specific, still not hype-driven — matches `BRAND_POSITIONING.md`'s tone even when the job is direct-response.
- **Formats:** individual property spotlights, "list your property this week" pushes, seasonal/campaign-tied offers (`content/strategy/` campaign docs, Phase 4.2, not yet written).
- **Frequency:** the smallest pillar by volume (matches the sourced 15% promotional ceiling) — this is deliberate; over-indexing here is exactly the generic-brand mistake this system is built to avoid.
- **Metrics:** the real, bottom-line business metrics — registrations, listings created, inquiries, eventually CAC (`docs/DEPLOYMENT-ENVIRONMENT-INVENTORY.md`-adjacent business metrics, once real usage data exists).

## 3. Suggested pillar-mix starting ratio

Adapting the sourced 40/25/20/15 (`content/research/SOURCES.md` #12) to the six pillars above, given Aqar's early-stage reality (no real social-proof content bank yet):

| Pillar | Starting share | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Product & Insight | 30% | The core differentiator — deserves the largest single share |
| Real Estate Education | 25% | Matches the sourced "educational" weight closely — the reach/trust engine |
| Trust & Credibility | 20% | Replaces the sourced "social proof" bucket, scaled similarly, honestly |
| Local & Diaspora Life | 15% | Track-specific relevance content |
| Conversion & Offers | 7% | Below the sourced 15% ceiling deliberately — early-stage brand-building should not over-index on direct response yet |
| Company / Brand | 3% | Lowest by design — occasional, not filler |

This is a starting hypothesis, not a locked allocation — revisit once real performance data exists (`content/strategy/ANALYTICS_FRAMEWORK.md`, Phase 2.12, not yet written).

## What this deliberately avoids

- No pillar is content-for-content's-sake — every one traces to a named business objective and a named audience pain point from `PERSONAS.md`.
- No pillar relies on fabricated social proof, testimonials, or usage numbers.
- The mix intentionally under-weights direct promotion relative to education/trust, matching both the sourced research and the product's actual early-stage reality (nothing to oversell yet — the honest story is strong enough on its own).
