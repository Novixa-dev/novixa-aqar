# Platform Strategy

Phase 1.2. Built on the confirmed decision (2026-08-29): the Gulf-based Yemeni diaspora is **co-primary** alongside domestic Yemen, not a later expansion. This means two real platform tracks, run in parallel from day one — not one blended "MENA" approach, and not two fully separate teams either; the same content pillars (see `CONTENT_STRATEGY.md`) get adapted per track, per the repurposing model in `REPURPOSING_SYSTEM.md` (Phase 4.5, not yet written).

Source data for everything below: `content/research/SOURCES.md` #5–8, #12; synthesis in `content/research/RESEARCH_NOTES.md` §1–2.

---

## Track A — Domestic Yemen

**Reality:** 17.7% internet penetration, 15.9% social media penetration, low-bandwidth-tolerant by necessity. This is a small, hard-won audience — treat every reached person as valuable, not as a rounding error against Gulf-scale numbers.

| Tier | Platform | Why | Content approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Facebook (+ Messenger) | 19% of the population uses it — the single largest reachable platform in-country by a wide margin (6.55M users vs. Instagram's 2.7M) | Native Arabic posts, education/trust-led (per the 40/25/20/15 mix — see `CONTENT_STRATEGY.md`), low-bandwidth-friendly video (works at lower resolution, captions essential), Messenger as a direct-inquiry channel |
| **Primary** | WhatsApp | Not separately measured in the penetration data found, but consistently described across sources as *the* real-estate conversion channel in the region, and Yemen's mobile-connection rate (56.8%) is far higher than its internet-penetration rate — WhatsApp works over cellular data more forgivingly than data-heavy apps | Not a broadcast channel — a conversion/follow-up channel. Every domestic-facing CTA should offer a WhatsApp path. Requires real operational capacity to respond fast (flagged to the user in `PLAN.md` open questions) |
| **Secondary** | Instagram | Real but smaller reach (7.9%) — worth a presence for visual property/brand content, not the primary bet | Property visuals, brand identity content — lower posting cadence than Track B's Instagram |
| **Experimental** | TikTok | No clean Yemen-specific usage figure was found this research pass (flagged as an open gap in `RESEARCH_NOTES.md`) — treat as untested until a real number exists | Don't invest production budget here for Track A until penetration is actually confirmed; monitor, don't commit |
| **Not prioritized (Track A)** | LinkedIn, X/Twitter, Snapchat, Pinterest | No evidence found supporting meaningful domestic-Yemen consumer reach for a real-estate audience on these | Revisit only if a specific B2B use case (Developer/Agency persona, LinkedIn) justifies it independently — see Track B |

## Track B — Gulf-Based Yemeni Diaspora

**Reality:** full Gulf-level digital reach (wherever they live — Saudi Arabia, UAE primarily, per remittance-source data), higher disposable income, already a demonstrated real-estate buyer.

| Tier | Platform | Why | Content approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | WhatsApp | Described as *the* Gulf business-communication channel, not merely a channel — real transactions reportedly close here; click-to-WhatsApp ads convert 2–3× vs. lead forms in the region | Remote-ownership trust content, fast-response conversion path, WhatsApp Business features (templates, automated sequences) worth investing in operationally, not just as a content afterthought |
| **Primary** | Instagram | The GCC-standard primary platform (UAE #1, KSA #2) — where a Gulf-based, income-bearing diaspora audience actually spends attention | Higher production value than Track A is realistic here (better average bandwidth/device quality); property showcases, remote-investment education, "manage your Yemen property from [Gulf city]" narrative content |
| **Secondary** | TikTok | KSA's #1 platform overall — a meaningful share of the diaspora audience likely lives in or near this market | Faster, trend-adapted, entertainment-leaning content — distinct tone from the more polished Instagram track |
| **Secondary** | LinkedIn | Named as the GCC's primary B2B lead-gen platform | Relevant specifically for the Developer/Agency/Business-customer personas (`PERSONAS.md` #7–8) within this track, not the general diaspora-investor persona |
| **Experimental** | Snapchat | Dominant specifically in KSA per the research, but its fit for a real-estate-investment (vs. general consumer) audience is unverified | Small-scale test only if/when Track B scales past initial content, not a day-one commitment |
| **Not prioritized** | X/Twitter, Pinterest, Facebook | No strong evidence found that these outperform the platforms above for this specific track's audience or product category | Revisit if a specific need emerges (e.g., X for real-time market-news-style content) |

## What both tracks share

- **YouTube** sits outside the Track A/B split — it's a *content-type* decision (long-form product education, deep explainers, the brand-introduction flagship piece) more than an audience-track decision, and per the research (Dec-2025 algorithm change), it works best fed by Shorts-first discovery from whichever track's short-form content is driving it. Treat YouTube as the shared long-form home for content atomized from either track's pillar content.
- **The underlying content pillars are the same across both tracks** (see `CONTENT_STRATEGY.md`) — what changes is platform mix, production polish level, dialect/tone calibration (`LANGUAGE_STRATEGY.md`), and which persona's pain points get emphasized.
- **Trust is the dominant cross-track theme** (per `PERSONAS.md`'s cross-persona notes) — both tracks need it, for different reasons (local-market trust for Track A, remote-verification trust for Track B).

## What this deliberately does NOT do

- It does not spread thin across every platform "just in case." Both tracks name explicit non-priorities.
- It does not treat "GCC/MENA" as one audience — the whole point of this document is that it isn't, for this specific product and market.
- It does not commit production budget to unverified channels (TikTok-Track-A, Snapchat) ahead of real data.
